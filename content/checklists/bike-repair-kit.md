# Bike repair kit

Most breakdowns on a bike are small — a puncture, a loose bolt, a slipped
chain — and fixable at the roadside with a few light tools. Carry the
ride kit every time, keep the home kit together in one box, and check both
each season. How to use them is in [[mobility-bike-maintenance]].

> **Tip:** Check your bike's bolt and valve sizes before you buy anything —
> tools that don't fit your bike are dead weight.

## Ride kit (carry on every ride)

- [ ] Spare inner tube in the right size and valve type
- [ ] Puncture repair kit (patches, glue or self-adhesive patches, sandpaper)
- [ ] Two or three tyre levers
- [ ] Mini pump that fits your valve type
- [ ] Multi-tool with the Allen keys and screwdriver your bike needs
- [ ] Chain tool or a multi-tool that includes one, plus a quick link
- [ ] A few zip ties and a strip of tape wrapped round the pump
- [ ] A rag or gloves for oily hands
- [ ] Front and rear lights, charged, or spare batteries

## Home kit

- [ ] Track pump with a pressure gauge
- [ ] Full set of Allen keys and spanners for your bike's bolts
- [ ] Chain lube, degreaser, brush, and rags
- [ ] Spare brake pads to suit your brakes
- [ ] Spare brake and gear cables and cable ends
- [ ] Spoke key, and cone spanners if you service hubs
- [ ] Spare tubes, patches, and a spare tyre
- [ ] Spare chain quick links
- [ ] A bucket of water for finding slow punctures
- [ ] A hook or stand to hold the bike off the ground

## Seasonal check

- [ ] Tyres pumped to the pressure on the sidewall; worn or cut tyres
      replaced
- [ ] Brakes stop firmly; pads not worn to the line
- [ ] Chain cleaned and lubed; not stretched
- [ ] Wheels spin straight; no loose spokes
- [ ] Bolts on handlebars, stem, saddle, and racks tight
- [ ] Lights and bell working
- [ ] Glue in the puncture kit not dried out
