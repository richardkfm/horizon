# Accessible emergency plan

Fill this in together with the person it's for: they know their own needs
best, and they decide what goes in it. Keep one copy with them, one with each
named helper, and review it every six months. For organising a street-wide
roster, see [[cooperation-care-for-neighbours]].

> **Tip:** For anyone relying on powered medical equipment, also work through
> [[health-power-dependent-care]].

## People

- [ ] Two named helpers who live close, with phone numbers
- [ ] A check-in rhythm agreed (daily call, weekly visit) and extra checks in
      heat, cold, storms, and power cuts
- [ ] A sign for "please knock" (a window card or agreed signal)
- [ ] Who has a spare key, and when they may use it
- [ ] What happens if there's no answer, and who to call
- [ ] An emergency contact and their wishes if they can't speak for
      themselves

## Getting the warning

- [ ] How they'll be alerted: a knock, text, call, flashing light, or
      vibrating alarm
- [ ] Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms suited to their hearing or sight
- [ ] A battery or wind-up radio they can use

## Evacuation

- [ ] Two ways out of the home, and which one works for them
- [ ] A plan if the lift stops or the stairs can't be used
- [ ] Helpers know how to move them safely, and have practised with their
      wheelchair or walking aid
- [ ] An accessible place to go: step-free, with a toilet they can use
- [ ] Transport arranged for getting there
- [ ] Assistance animals included, with their food and lead

## Equipment and supplies

- [ ] Walking aid, wheelchair, or scooter, with a puncture kit or spare tyre
- [ ] Glasses, hearing aids, and spare batteries
- [ ] Chargers and a power bank for phones and equipment
- [ ] Backup for power-dependent equipment (batteries, manual alternatives)
- [ ] Continence supplies and spare clothes for at least three days
- [ ] Dentures, communication aids, and any picture or symbol cards

## Health and care

- [ ] Medicine list and at least a week's supply (see
      [[checklist:household-medicines]])
- [ ] Care routines written down (toileting, skin checks, turning, eating
      and drinking help)
- [ ] For someone living with dementia: what calms them, familiar objects,
      and the names they use for people and things
- [ ] Contact details for their care workers, nurse, or doctor

## Review

- [ ] Plan checked together every six months
- [ ] Helpers have a current copy
- [ ] Practised at least once a year
