# Day pack for walks and outdoor trips

Most outdoor trouble starts small — a wrong turn, a twisted ankle, a weather
change, a day that runs into dusk. A light, well-packed day bag turns those
into stories rather than emergencies. Check it before each trip and leave
word of your route with someone at home.

> **Tip:** Learn to use what you carry before you need it — see
> [[survival-map-and-compass]], [[survival-knots]], and
> [[survival-signaling-for-rescue]].

## Before you go

- [ ] Route and expected return time left with someone at home
- [ ] Weather forecast checked; plans changed if it's bad
- [ ] Phone charged; offline maps downloaded (see
      [[survival-offline-maps-and-gps]])

## Navigation and communication

- [ ] Paper map of the area in a waterproof bag
- [ ] Compass
- [ ] Phone, and a small charged power bank with cable
- [ ] Whistle (three blasts means help)
- [ ] Head torch with spare batteries

## Water and food

- [ ] At least 1–2 litres of water per person, more in heat
- [ ] A small filter or purification tablets
- [ ] Lunch and extra snacks that need no cooking

## Clothing

- [ ] Waterproof jacket
- [ ] Warm layer and a hat, even in summer
- [ ] Sun hat, sunglasses, and sun cream
- [ ] Spare socks
- [ ] Sturdy, broken-in footwear

## First aid and safety

- [ ] Small first-aid kit: plasters, blister dressings, bandage, gloves,
      tape (see [[checklist:first-aid-kit]])
- [ ] Personal medicines, including any inhaler or allergy kit
- [ ] Emergency foil blanket or survival bag
- [ ] Insect repellent

## Useful extras

- [ ] Pocket knife or multi-tool
- [ ] A length of cord
- [ ] Lighter or waterproof matches
- [ ] A small bag to carry out rubbish
- [ ] Some cash and an ID card
- [ ] Notebook and pencil
