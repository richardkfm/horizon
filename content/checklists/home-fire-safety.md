# Home fire safety

Most home fires start in the kitchen, from heaters and candles, or from
overloaded electrics — and most deaths are from smoke, at night. Working
alarms and a practised escape plan are what save lives. Go through this list
once a year, and test the alarms every month.

> **Tip:** Read [[emergency-extinguish-fire]] for tackling a small fire, and
> [[shelter-weatherproof-home]] for keeping a sealed-up home safely
> ventilated.

## Alarms

- [ ] A smoke alarm on every floor, in hallways and landings
- [ ] Ideally linked alarms, so one going off sets them all off
- [ ] A heat alarm in the kitchen (smoke alarms there go off with toast)
- [ ] A carbon monoxide alarm in every room with a fuel-burning appliance
- [ ] Alarms tested monthly with the test button
- [ ] Batteries changed yearly, or 10-year sealed alarms fitted
- [ ] A vibrating or flashing alarm for anyone who is deaf or hard of hearing

## Kitchen

- [ ] Never leave cooking unattended, especially oil
- [ ] A fire blanket mounted near (not over) the cooker
- [ ] Pan handles turned inwards, tea towels away from the hob
- [ ] Grease cleaned from the cooker, extractor, and filters

## Heating, chimneys, and candles

- [ ] Chimneys and flues swept at least once a year if used
- [ ] Only dry, seasoned wood burned in a stove or fire
- [ ] A fireguard in front of any open fire
- [ ] Heaters at least 1 m from curtains, furniture, and drying clothes
- [ ] Candles in stable holders, away from curtains, never left burning
      unattended
- [ ] Matches and lighters kept out of children's reach
- [ ] Appliance air vents never blocked

## Electrics and batteries

- [ ] No overloaded sockets or chains of adaptors
- [ ] No cables under rugs or carpets; damaged cables replaced
- [ ] Chargers and appliances unplugged when not in use and at night
- [ ] Phones and power banks charged on hard surfaces, not on beds
- [ ] Swollen or damaged batteries removed (see [[energy-everyday-batteries]])

## Escape plan

- [ ] Two ways out of every room where possible, agreed with everyone
- [ ] Exits and hallways kept clear
- [ ] Door and window keys kept in an agreed place near each exit
- [ ] An outside meeting place everyone knows
- [ ] A plan for babies, children, older or disabled people, and pets
- [ ] Escape practised at least twice a year, including in the dark
- [ ] Interior doors closed at night to slow smoke and fire

## Firefighting kit

- [ ] A fire extinguisher suitable for home use, in date and checked
- [ ] Everyone knows how to use it (PASS: pull, aim, squeeze, sweep)
- [ ] Everyone knows the rule: if it's bigger than a wastebasket, get out
