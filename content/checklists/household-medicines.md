# Household medicines and care plan

Fill this in once for each person in the household, keep it with their
medicines, and put a copy in the go-bag. Review it every few months, and
whenever a prescription changes. The full how-to is in
[[health-household-medicines]].

> **Tip:** Paper still works when phones and the power don't. Write the list
> by hand if you need to — it only has to be readable.

## For each person

- [ ] Name, date of birth, and any allergies (and what happens)
- [ ] Every regular medicine: name, strength, dose, times, and what it's for
- [ ] Over-the-counter medicines and supplements they take
- [ ] Long-term conditions (diabetes, asthma, epilepsy, heart, mental health)
- [ ] Prescriber, pharmacy, and clinic contact details
- [ ] Vaccination dates, including the last tetanus booster
- [ ] Glasses, hearing aids, and spare batteries noted
- [ ] Medical equipment that needs power or supplies noted (see
      [[health-power-dependent-care]])
- [ ] Who to contact, and their wishes, if they can't speak for themselves

## Storage

- [ ] Medicines in a cool, dry cupboard — not the bathroom, not by the cooker
- [ ] Adult medicines high up or locked, out of children's reach and sight
- [ ] Everything kept in original packaging with the leaflet
- [ ] Fridge medicines in the main body of the fridge, never frozen
- [ ] A plan for keeping fridge medicines cool in a power cut
- [ ] A weekly pill box for anyone on several medicines a day

## Refills

- [ ] Days of supply left written on each person's list
- [ ] Reorder dates marked on a shared calendar
- [ ] Reorder with one to two weeks in hand (more before holidays or winter)
- [ ] Asked the prescriber about a small reserve of essential medicines
- [ ] A few days' supply packed in the go-bag, rotated so it stays in date

## Checks every few months

- [ ] Out-of-date medicines removed, especially liquids, eye drops, insulin,
      and emergency medicines
- [ ] Emergency medicines (inhalers, adrenaline auto-injectors) in date and
      where everyone knows to find them
- [ ] Old medicines returned to a pharmacy, not flushed or binned loose
- [ ] Sharps in a rigid, lidded sharps container
- [ ] The household knows never to share prescription medicines
- [ ] Local crisis line and emergency numbers written on the list
