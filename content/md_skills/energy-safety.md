# Energy answer safety

Apply this whenever an answer involves solar, wind, batteries, wiring,
backup power, heating, or any other home energy setup.

- [ ] **Renewable first.** Lead with solar, wind, small hydro, human or muscle
      power, and biogas. Mention a fuel generator only as a last resort, and
      always with the carbon monoxide rules below.
- [ ] **Fuse every positive battery lead, close to the battery.** A fuse (or
      breaker) sized to the cable goes within a few centimetres of the
      battery's positive terminal, so a short anywhere downstream can't turn
      the cable into a heating element.
- [ ] **Use the right wire gauge.** Size cable for the current and the run
      length; thin cable overheats and loses power. Keep low-voltage runs
      short and say when a thicker cable is needed.
- [ ] **Lithium needs a BMS.** Never suggest charging lithium or LiFePO₄
      batteries without a battery management system, and never charge them
      below 0 °C — it damages the cells permanently and can make them unsafe.
- [ ] **No combustion indoors.** Never run a generator, engine, barbecue, or
      unvented fuel heater indoors, in a garage, or near windows and vents.
      Keep generators at least 6 m (20 ft) away with the exhaust pointing
      away, and recommend a battery carbon monoxide alarm.
- [ ] **Mains is for a qualified electrician.** Anything connected to the
      grid or the home's mains wiring — grid-tie inverters, connecting a
      generator or battery to the house circuits — must be done by a qualified
      electrician, with the right isolation switch so power can't feed back
      into the lines and harm line workers.
- [ ] **Isolate before working.** Switch off and disconnect the source (and
      cover solar panels, which make power whenever light falls on them)
      before touching any wiring, and check it's dead.
- [ ] **Be honest about size.** Show the sizing method (daily Wh, sun hours,
      ÷ 0.7 for losses) rather than a bare number, and say that real use is
      usually higher than the first estimate.
- [ ] **Cite local guides** (the energy guides on sizing, low-tech solar,
      battery storage, wind, and solar water heating) for the practical steps.
