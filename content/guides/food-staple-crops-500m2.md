# Grow staple crops on 500 m²

A 500 m² plot, well used, can provide a meaningful share of a small group's
calories. The goal here is *calories and storability*, not variety — focus on a
few reliable staples suited to your climate.

> **Risk:** Soil contamination (heavy metals, sewage) makes food unsafe. If you
> are unsure of a site's history, test the soil before growing food on it.

## Materials

- Seeds or tubers of locally-proven staples (e.g. potatoes, beans, maize, squash)
- Basic hand tools (fork, hoe, rake)
- Compost or other organic matter
- Mulch material

## Procedure

1. **Assess the site:** sunlight, drainage, and soil. Aim for 6+ hours of sun.
2. **Build the soil:** add compost and organic matter; avoid leaving soil bare.
3. **Choose staples for your climate** and stagger planting to spread the harvest.
4. **Plant at the depth and spacing in the table below.**
5. **Mulch** to retain moisture and suppress weeds.
6. **Water deeply but less often** to encourage strong roots.
7. **Harvest and cure** for storage (e.g. cure potatoes and squash before storing).

## Planting and yield, crop by crop

Each block in the layout below is about 83 m². The figures are for a decent
year on ordinary soil; a first year on poor ground gives less.

| Crop | When to plant | Depth | Spacing | For one block |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Potatoes | Early spring, 2–4 weeks before the last frost | 10–15 cm | 30 cm apart, rows 75 cm apart | About 20 kg of seed tubers |
| Dry beans | After the last frost, in warm soil | 3–5 cm | 10 cm apart, rows 50 cm apart | About 0.8 kg of seed |
| Maize for grain | After the last frost, in warm soil | 3–5 cm | 25–30 cm apart, rows 75 cm apart | About 0.2 kg of seed |
| Winter squash | After the last frost | 2–3 cm | 1–1.5 m apart, rows 2 m apart | About 100 seeds (2–3 per spot, thin to the strongest) |

| Crop | Rough yield per m² | One block gives | How it keeps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Potatoes | 2–3 kg | 170–250 kg | Cure 1–2 weeks, then cool and dark: 4–8 months |
| Dry beans | 0.15–0.25 kg | 12–20 kg | Dried hard, in a sealed jar: years |
| Maize for grain | 0.3–0.6 kg | 25–50 kg | Dried on the cob, then shelled: a year or more |
| Winter squash | 2–4 kg | 150–300 kg | Cure 2 weeks somewhere warm, then cool and dry: 3–6 months |

> **Note:** Be honest about what this feeds. Four blocks of these crops give
> very roughly half of what one adult eats in a year. Plant all six blocks
> and it is closer to one adult's needs. That is a real share of a small
> group's food, not the whole of it.

Move each crop to the next block every year, so pests and diseases don't
build up in the soil. For storing the harvest, see [[food-root-cellar]].

## Seed saving

- Let a few of your strongest plants go to seed.
- Dry seeds fully and store them cool, dark, and dry, labelled with the date.

*Fig. 1: one way to lay out a 500 m² plot — a block of wind-pollinated maize,
mixed beds for the rest, a staging area for compost, and a row left to seed*

Blocks E and F are generous while you are starting out. Once the compost is
established, shrink them to a strip along one edge and plant the ground
they free up.
