# Run a composting or dry toilet

A flush toilet uses clean drinking water to carry waste away — often a
quarter or more of a household's water. A dry toilet uses none. Done well, it
doesn't smell, it keeps waste safely contained, and over time it turns into
compost that can feed trees. Allotments, cabins, festivals, and many eco-homes
run them every day, and they keep working when the water or sewer doesn't.

> **Risk:** Human faeces carry germs and worm eggs that cause serious
> illness. Fresh or half-composted material must never touch food crops,
> drinking water, or bare hands. Rules on composting toilets and using their
> compost vary between countries and councils — check local guidance before
> building one or using what it makes.

## How it works

Two things make a dry toilet work: **keep urine and faeces apart**, and
**cover every deposit** with dry, carbon-rich material.

- **Urine diversion:** a seat or insert with a separate front bowl sends
  urine to its own container or soakaway. Faeces stay much drier, so they
  don't smell and break down better.
- **Cover material:** a scoop of sawdust, dry leaves, shredded cardboard,
  coconut coir, or chopped straw after each use. It soaks up moisture, blocks
  smell, keeps flies off, and adds the carbon that composting needs.

## Build or set one up

- **Simplest — a bucket toilet:** a sturdy bucket with a seat and lid, a
  sack of sawdust beside it, and a lidded bin or outdoor compost bay to empty
  it into. Fine for a household starting out.
- **Two-chamber (vault) toilet:** two sealed, watertight chambers under one
  seat, built above ground or with a sealed floor, so nothing leaks into the
  soil. The seat sits over one chamber at a time.
- **Ventilation:** a vent pipe from the chamber to above the roofline, with
  a fly screen on top. Air flows down through the seat and out of the pipe,
  so the room doesn't smell.
- **Fly-proof everything:** a closed lid when not in use, screened vents, and
  tight hatches.

## Site it well

> **Decision:** Keep the toilet and any compost from it **well away from
> every water source** — at least 30 m from wells, springs, streams, and
> rainwater tanks, more on sandy or rocky ground, and always downhill of them.
> For an unsealed pit in the ground, use the wider distances in
> [[survival-sanitation-hygiene]].

- Use watertight chambers or a sealed floor, so liquid can't soak into
  groundwater.
- Put it where rain can't flood it and where you can reach the hatches to
  empty them.

## Use it every day

1. **Sit down to use it** — everyone, so urine goes to the front bowl.
2. **Cover after each use** with a good scoop of cover material until no
   faeces are visible.
3. **Toilet paper** can go in with the faeces; nothing else — no wipes,
   nappies, period products, or plastics.
4. **Close the lid.**
5. **Wash your hands with soap** every time. Keep a handwashing station (a
   tap, a jug and basin, or a tippy-tap) right by the door.
6. **Keep it clean:** wipe the seat and bowl with a mild cleaner. Avoid bleach
   in the chamber — it harms composting.

## Handle urine

Urine from healthy people carries far fewer germs than faeces and is rich in
nitrogen, a plant food.

- **Simplest:** send it to a soakaway (a gravel-filled pit) or a dense bed of
  shrubs or trees, well away from water sources.
- **As a fertiliser:** store it in a sealed container for at least a month,
  then dilute it — roughly 1 part urine to 8–10 parts water — and pour it on
  the soil, never on leaves. Stop at least a month before harvesting any crop,
  and don't use it on food eaten raw close to harvest.

## Rotate, rest, and cure

1. **Use chamber A** until it's about three-quarters full — often several
   months to a year for a household.
2. **Top it with a thick layer of cover material, seal it,** and label it
   with the date.
3. **Switch the seat to chamber B** and carry on.
4. **Leave chamber A resting** for **at least a year** — longer in a cool
   climate. Time, dryness, and soil life kill most germs.
5. **Empty A only when B is nearly full,** then switch back.

> **Spec:** After at least a year's rest, the material should look and smell
> like dark, crumbly earth with nothing recognisable. Even then, use it **only
> around trees, shrubs, and non-food plants** — dig it in, don't leave it on
> the surface, and never put it on vegetable beds. Follow any stricter local
> rules.

When emptying a chamber:

- Wear gloves, long sleeves, and closed shoes. Use a dedicated shovel.
- Move it straight to where it's going and dig it in.
- Wash tools, gloves, and hands thoroughly afterwards.

## Troubleshooting

- **Smell:** too wet or not enough cover. Add more cover material, check
  urine isn't getting into the chamber, and check the vent is clear.
- **Flies:** gaps in screens or lids, or deposits left uncovered. Seal gaps
  and cover thoroughly.
- **Too wet:** add a layer of dry leaves or sawdust and fix any rain leak.
