# Reuse greywater safely in the garden

Greywater is the lightly used water from showers, baths, hand basins, and the
washing machine. A household sends tens of litres of it down the drain every
day — water that could be growing fruit trees and shrubs instead. Reusing it
saves treated water, cuts bills, and keeps a garden green through dry spells,
in ordinary times as much as in a drought.

The idea is simple: get the water **straight into mulch-covered soil, near the
roots, the same day**, so the soil's own life cleans it before anyone or
anything else touches it.

> **Risk:** Greywater carries skin bacteria, soap, lint, and sometimes traces
> of faeces (from washing bodies and clothes). Treat it as unsafe to drink and
> unsafe on food you eat raw. Never let it pool, run off your land, or reach a
> well, spring, or stream. Rules on greywater differ a lot between countries
> and councils — check local guidance before building anything permanent.

## What you can and can't reuse

| Source | Reuse? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shower, bath, hand basin | Yes | Lightly soiled, the best source |
| Washing machine (rinse and wash) | Yes | Plenty of water; mind the detergent |
| Kitchen sink, dishwasher | No | Grease and food scraps clog soil and attract pests |
| Water from washing nappies | No | Contains faeces — send it to the sewer or septic tank |
| Toilet water (blackwater) | Never | Sewage; needs proper treatment |
| Water with bleach, dye, solvents, or paint | No | Harms soil life and plants |
| Water softener backwash | No | Very salty |

> **Tip:** When someone in the house is ill with diarrhoea or vomiting, send
> all greywater to the drain until they've been well for a few days.

## Choose plant-friendly soaps

What goes into the drain ends up in your soil, so switch products first —
this one change matters more than any pipe.

- **Low salt (sodium):** liquid detergents are usually far lower in salt than
  powders, which often use sodium salts as filler. Salt builds up and slowly
  poisons soil.
- **No boron or borax:** plants need only a trace of boron, and too much is
  toxic to them. Check the label.
- **No chlorine bleach** or "antibacterial" additives — they kill the soil
  microbes that clean the water.
- **Simple and biodegradable:** plain plant-based soaps, mild shampoos, and
  eco laundry liquids. Use the least that gets the job done.

## Never store it

> **Spec:** Use greywater **within 24 hours**. Warm, soapy water left in a
> tank or bucket goes septic and smelly within a day or two, and bacteria
> multiply fast. A greywater system moves water straight to the soil; it is
> not a storage system.

If you're carrying water by bucket from a bath or basin, tip it into a mulch
basin around a tree the same day. Don't keep it "for later".

## Build a branched drain to mulch basins

A branched drain is the simplest permanent system: no pump, no filter, no
tank. Gravity carries water downhill and splits it, again and again, into
several mulch basins around trees and shrubs.

```ascii
   house drain (shower / laundry)
            |
            | gentle fall: ~2 cm per metre
            v
        +---+---+        <- flow splitter (double elbow)
        |       |
        v       v
     +--+--+  +-+---+    <- split again if you need
     |     |  |     |       more outlets
     v     v  v     v
   (o)   (o) (o)   (o)   <- outlets in mulch basins
   tree  tree bush  bush    around the drip line

    side view of one basin:

     outlet under a cover        tree
            |                     |
   ~~~~~~~~[_]~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~|~~~~~~ soil level
          \   wood-chip mulch   / |
           \___________________/  |
             water soaks in     roots
```

*Fig. 1: a branched drain splits greywater by gravity into mulch basins
around trees; each outlet sits in a covered chamber inside a wood-chip-filled
basin, so the water soaks in under mulch and is never exposed*

1. **Pick the plants:** fruit and nut trees, berry bushes, ornamentals, or
   large shrubs — plants with deep roots that use a lot of water.
2. **Dig a basin** around each plant at the drip line (under the edge of the
   canopy), 20–30 cm deep, not right against the trunk.
3. **Fill it with wood chips or coarse mulch** — the mulch filters lint, keeps
   the water underground, and feeds soil life that breaks down soap.
4. **Lay the pipe** with a steady downhill fall the whole way (no low spots
   where water sits). Use splitters to divide the flow between basins.
5. **Cover each outlet** with a small upturned bucket, a pot, or a box, so
   the water drops into mulch and can't be touched or splashed.
6. **Fit a simple diverter valve** near the house so you can send water to the
   sewer or septic tank in wet weather, when someone is ill, or when you've
   used a product that isn't garden-safe.

> **Pick this if:** you have a garden lower than the house and want something
> that runs itself. **A laundry-to-landscape line** — the machine's own pump
> pushing water through a pipe to several basins — works on flat ground too.

## Keep it away from food and people

- **Apply under mulch or soil only.** Never spray it through a sprinkler or
  hose it onto leaves — the fine mist carries germs.
- **Keep it off the edible parts of plants.** Water the roots of fruit trees,
  where the fruit hangs far above the ground. Don't use it on leafy greens,
  herbs, or root crops (carrots, potatoes) that you eat raw or that touch the
  soil.
- **Keep it away from water sources** — well clear of any well, spring,
  stream, or rainwater tank; check local rules for exact distances.
- **Keep children and pets off** the basins; the covered outlets help.

## Watch for warning signs

- **Pooling, smell, or soggy ground** → too much water for the basin. Add
  more basins or send some to the drain.
- **Yellowing or burnt leaf edges** → salt or boron build-up. Check your
  products, rest that basin, and let rain flush the soil.
- **Slow flow** → lint or roots in the pipe; clear it and top up the mulch.
- **Rotate and rest basins** in the rainy season, and refresh the mulch each
  year as it breaks down into rich soil.
