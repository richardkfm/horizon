# Look after everyday batteries

Torches, radios, phones, power banks, smoke alarms — everyday life runs on
small batteries. A little care makes them last years longer, saves money and
waste, and means the torch actually works when the lights go out. It also
keeps you safe: damaged lithium batteries can catch fire.

> **Risk:** A **swollen, hot, hissing, leaking, or smoking** battery is
> dangerous. Stop using and charging it straight away. If it's safe to touch,
> move it outdoors or onto a non-flammable surface (stone, a metal tray, a
> bucket of sand) away from anything that can burn. Never puncture, squash, or
> try to charge it. If it catches fire, get out and call the fire service.

## AA and AAA cells: alkaline or rechargeable?

| | Alkaline (single-use) | NiMH rechargeable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voltage | 1.5 V | 1.2 V — fine for almost all devices |
| Best for | Smoke alarms, clocks, remotes, emergency torches stored for years | Torches, radios, toys, cameras — anything used often |
| Shelf life | Many years | "Low self-discharge" types keep most of their charge for a year or more |
| Cold weather | Weak below freezing | Better than alkaline |
| Recharge? | **Never** — they can leak or burst | Hundreds of times |

> **Pick this if:** you use a device every week — get **low self-discharge
> NiMH** cells (often sold as "pre-charged" or "ready to use") and a smart
> charger that stops when they're full. They pay for themselves quickly and
> cut waste. Keep a sealed pack of alkalines for the emergency kit.

### Care for AA and AAA cells

- **Don't mix** old with new, or different types or brands, in one device.
- **Take batteries out** of anything you won't use for months — leaking
  alkalines ruin the device.
- **Clean corroded contacts** with a cotton bud dipped in vinegar or lemon
  juice, then dry them.
- **Store loose cells** in a box or with tape over the ends, so they can't
  short against keys or coins.
- **Charge NiMH in a proper charger,** never in a device meant for alkalines.

## Phones, power banks, and other lithium batteries

Lithium-ion batteries hold a lot of energy in a small space. They wear out
faster when kept very full, very empty, or hot.

```ascii
   charge level for a lithium battery

   100% |##########|  fine for today, but avoid sitting
        |##########|  here for weeks
    80% |########  |  <- everyday sweet spot: 20-80%
        |######    |
    60% |######    |  <- storage zone: 40-60%
    40% |####      |     for anything put away
        |##        |
    20% |##        |  <- recharge before here
     0% |          |  avoid running flat
```

*Fig. 1: lithium battery charge levels — daily use between about 20% and 80%
is gentlest; store spare devices and power banks at about half charge*

- **Everyday:** recharge before it falls very low, and don't worry about
  stopping before 100%. Many phones have a setting that limits charging to
  80% — use it.
- **Storage:** charge power banks and spare devices to **about 40–60%**
  before putting them away. Check and top up every 3–6 months.
- **Heat is the enemy:** don't leave phones or power banks in a hot car, in
  direct sun, or charging under a pillow. Charge on a hard surface with air
  around it.
- **Cold:** batteries give less power in the cold. Keep your phone in an
  inner pocket in winter.

> **Avoid if:** the battery is **below 0 °C**. Don't charge lithium batteries
> when they're freezing — it damages them permanently and can make them
> unsafe. Bring them indoors and let them warm to room temperature first.

- **Use a decent charger and cable** that suits the device. Cheap,
  unbranded chargers are a common cause of battery fires.
- **Don't charge overnight** next to where people sleep if you can avoid it,
  and never charge a battery that's been dropped and dented.

## Button and coin batteries

> **Do now:** If a child may have **swallowed a button or coin battery**, go
> to emergency care immediately, even if they seem fine. It can burn through
> the throat or gut within hours. Don't make them vomit.

Keep button batteries, and devices with loose battery covers, well out of
children's reach. Tape over the terminals of used ones before disposal.

## Dispose of batteries safely

- **Never put batteries in household rubbish or recycling.** Lithium
  batteries crushed in bin lorries and sorting plants start fires.
- **Tape over the terminals** of lithium, 9 V, and button batteries before
  disposal.
- **Take them to a battery collection point** — many shops, supermarkets, and
  recycling centres have one. Check local guidance.
- **Keep used batteries** in a lidded box (not metal) until you take them in.
