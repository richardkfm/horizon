# Make fair group decisions

Rebuilding is a group effort. How a group decides is as important as what it
decides: fair, transparent processes build the trust that keeps people
cooperating voluntarily.

> **Note:** Prefer consent and broad agreement over domination by the loud
> or the powerful — and over a simple majority vote, which can steamroll the
> people who most need to be heard. No one should be coerced; everyone
> affected should have a real voice. Consensus is slower and harder, and it
> is worth it.

## A simple decision process

1. **Frame the decision** clearly: what are we deciding, and why now?
2. **Gather perspectives:** make space for quieter voices before louder ones —
   go round the circle so everyone speaks once before anyone speaks twice.
3. **Surface proposals** and the needs behind them.
4. **Test for consent:** ask not "who's in favour?" but "can you live with this,
   and if not, what's your concern?"
5. **Integrate objections** into an improved proposal where possible, then
   test for consent again.
6. **Decide and record** what was agreed, who stood aside (see below), who
   does what, and when to review it.

## Agree, stand aside, or block

When you test for consent, each person has three honest answers:

| Answer | What it means | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Agree** | "I can live with this." | Counts towards consent. |
| **Stand aside** | "I won't block this, but I won't help carry it out." | The decision goes ahead; the stand-aside and the reason are **written in the record**, so the concern isn't lost. |
| **Block** | "This goes against something fundamental for the group, and I object on principle." | The decision **does not go ahead** as it stands; the group goes back to step 5. |

> **Tip:** A block is a serious step, used rarely — for a principled objection
> (harm to people, breaking the group's agreed values), not a personal
> preference. If one person blocks often, talk about it with them outside the
> meeting. If several people stand aside, treat it as a warning: the proposal
> probably needs more work even though it can pass.

> **Decision:** If the group really can't reach consent and a decision can't
> wait, fall back only on a method the whole group agreed **in advance, by
> consent** (for example a large supermajority, or a small trial with a review
> date) — never a simple majority vote invented on the spot.

## When it's a dispute, not a decision

If the disagreement is really between particular people — hurt feelings,
broken trust, a clash of personalities — no decision process will fix it.
Use [[cooperation-resolving-conflict]] instead. If someone is being pressured
or threatened, it's a safety issue: see [[cooperation-boundary-safety]].

## When it's about who gets what

Decisions about pooled tools, food, land and labour come up again and again.
Rather than re-deciding each time, agree a standing system once — see
[[cooperation-sharing-resources]].
