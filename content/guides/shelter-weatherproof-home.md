# Weatherproof and retrofit the home you live in

You don't need to build a new house to live in a warm, dry, cheap-to-run one.
Most homes leak heat through gaps, an under-insulated loft, and bare windows,
and simple fixes — many of them free or nearly so — make a real difference
to comfort and bills. The warmest, cleanest energy is the energy you no longer
need, and a snug home also rides out a cold snap or a power cut far better.

> **Risk:** Seal draughts, **not ventilation.** Every home needs fresh air to
> stop damp and mould, and any fuel-burning appliance (boiler, gas cooker,
> stove, fire) needs air to burn safely — without it, deadly carbon monoxide
> builds up. Never block air bricks, trickle vents, or appliance vents. Fit a
> carbon monoxide alarm in every room with a fuel-burning appliance.

## Start with a draught hunt

On a cold, windy day, walk round with a lit incense stick or a strip of thin
paper, or just the back of a damp hand, and feel for moving air.

```ascii
       +------- loft hatch: seal + insulate lid
       |
    ___v__________________________
   | ^  ^  loft insulation  ^  ^ |
   |-----------------------------|
   | [window]        [window]    |<- gaps round frames:
   | curtains,       film,       |   strips + sealant
   | film            sealing     |
   |                             |<- pipe holes through
   | [door] keyhole, letterbox,  |   outside walls
   |        bottom gap           |
   |_______floorboard gaps_______|<- skirting boards
      ^ air brick: KEEP OPEN
```

*Fig. 1: where draughts get in — around doors, windows, the loft hatch,
floorboards, skirting boards, and holes for pipes; air bricks and vents are
there on purpose and stay open*

## Step 1: Draught-proof

- **Doors:** brush or rubber strips round the frame and along the bottom; a
  letterbox brush; a keyhole cover; a heavy curtain on the inside in winter.
- **Windows:** self-adhesive foam or rubber strips where opening parts close.
  Seal fixed gaps around frames with flexible sealant.
- **Floorboards and skirting boards:** fill gaps with flexible filler or
  thin strips; lay rugs.
- **Loft hatch:** a strip around the edge and insulation stuck to the top
  of the lid.
- **Unused chimneys:** a removable chimney draught excluder — but **only if
  nothing burns in that fireplace**, and take it out before lighting a fire.
  Leave a small gap for airflow to stop damp.
- **Pipe and cable holes** through outside walls: fill with expanding foam or
  sealant.

## Step 2: Insulate the loft

Heat rises, and an uninsulated loft can lose a quarter of a home's heat.

- **Lay mineral wool, sheep's wool, or similar rolls** between the joists,
  then a second layer across them. Recommended depths vary by country and
  climate — many cold-climate guides suggest about 25–30 cm in total; check
  local guidance.
- **Keep the eaves clear** so air can still move through the loft and it
  doesn't get damp.
- **Keep insulation away** from chimneys, flues, and recessed lights, and
  lay cables on top, not buried.
- **Insulate pipes and any water tank** in the loft — it's now colder up
  there and they could freeze. Don't insulate under the tank, so a little
  warmth rises to it.
- **Wear a mask, gloves, and long sleeves,** and walk only on boards across
  the joists.

## Step 3: Windows

- **Thick or lined curtains** that reach the sill or floor, closed at dusk,
  open on sunny days to let the sun in.
- **Window film:** clear plastic shrink film stuck around the frame and
  tightened with a hairdryer adds a still-air layer for a winter, cheaply.
- **Secondary glazing** panels are a bigger step and nearly as good as
  double glazing.
- **Blinds or bubble wrap** on rarely used windows.

## Step 4: Thermal zoning — heat the room you're in

- **Choose one or two rooms** to keep properly warm and spend the evening
  there together.
- **Close doors** to unheated rooms and hang a curtain over an open doorway
  or stairwell.
- **Move beds and sofas** away from cold outside walls.
- **Warm the person, not just the room:** layers, a hat, blankets, and hot
  drinks.

> **Tip:** Once the home is sealed and insulated, cleaner heating such as a
> heat pump or solar water heating can do the job — see
> [[energy-solar-water-heating]]. Insulation first, then heating; never the
> other way round.

## Keep ventilation working

- **Open windows for 10–15 minutes a day,** or more when cooking, drying
  clothes, or bathing.
- **Use extractor fans** in kitchens and bathrooms, and keep trickle vents
  open.
- **Dry laundry outside or near an open window**, not on radiators in a
  closed room.
- **Watch for damp:** condensation on windows every morning, mould in
  corners, or a musty smell mean you need more airflow — or there's a leak.

## Keep the rain out

- **Clear gutters and downpipes** in autumn.
- **Check the roof** from the ground with binoculars for slipped or missing
  tiles, and fix them before the next storm.
- **Seal around window frames** outside, and keep soil and paths below the
  damp-proof line of the walls.

## Home fire safety

- **Smoke alarms** on every floor, ideally linked, plus a heat alarm in the
  kitchen. Test them monthly; change batteries yearly (or fit 10-year
  sealed ones).
- **Chimneys and flues:** have them swept at least once a year if you use
  them — more often for wood. Burn only dry, seasoned wood.
- **Heaters and candles:** at least 1 m from curtains, furniture, and
  clothes; never leave candles or heaters on unattended.
- **Electrics:** don't overload sockets or run cables under rugs; unplug
  chargers and appliances at night.
- **An escape plan:** two ways out of every room where possible, keys kept by
  doors, a meeting place outside. Practise it twice a year, and close
  interior doors at night to slow a fire.
