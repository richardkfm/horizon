# Build a slow sand filter

A slow sand filter is a simple, low-tech way to make surface water much safer to
drink. Water trickles slowly through fine sand where a living biological layer
(the *schmutzdecke*) removes most pathogens.

> **Risk:** A slow sand filter reduces but does not guarantee removal of all
> contaminants. Always pair it with [[water-field-testing|water testing]] and,
> where possible, a final disinfection step (boiling, chlorine, or solar).
> Never assume output is safe without testing.

## Materials

- A clean food-grade barrel or tank (≈ 60–200 L)
- Fine, washed sand (the main filter bed)
- Coarse gravel (support layer)
- A perforated collector pipe for the bottom, plus solid pipe that rises up
  the outside of the barrel to the outlet (see below)
- A small valve or tap on the outlet to set the flow
- A diffuser plate (to avoid disturbing the sand surface)

## The outlet must rise above the sand

Water is collected at the bottom, but the outlet pipe has to **rise back up to
about 5 cm above the sand surface** before it pours out. Because water finds
its own level, the filter can then never drain below that point: the sand
always stays under water and the living layer on top stays alive, even when
nobody is feeding the filter. An outlet at the bottom lets the barrel drain
dry overnight and kills that layer.

```ascii
   inflow
     |
  +--v-------------------------+
  |~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~|  standing water
  |=========diffuser===========|
  |~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~|    +--> [tap]  outlet,
  |. . . . . . . . . . . . . . |    |    ~5 cm above sand
  |. . . .  fine sand  . . . . |    |
  |. . . .  >= 60 cm  . . . . .|    |  riser pipe
  |. . . . . . . . . . . . . . |    |
  | o o o  coarse gravel  o o o|    |
  | o ==perforated collector===+----+
  +----------------------------+
```

## Procedure

1. **Wash the sand and gravel** thoroughly until rinse water runs clear.
2. **Layer the bed:** gravel at the bottom around the perforated collector
   pipe, then a thick layer (≥ 60 cm) of fine sand on top.
3. **Fit the riser:** run solid pipe from the collector up the outside of the
   barrel so its outlet sits about 5 cm above the sand surface, with a small
   valve or tap on the end.
4. **Add a diffuser** above the sand so incoming water does not dig a hole.
5. **Charge the filter:** fill it from the top and keep water standing above
   the sand at all times.
6. **Ripen the filter:** run it continuously for **about 2–3 weeks** so the
   schmutzdecke develops. Until then, don't trust the output — and even
   afterwards, disinfect it before drinking (see the Risk note above).
7. **Run slowly:** aim for a filtration rate of roughly **0.1–0.4 m/h** —
   that is, the water above the sand would drop 10–40 cm an hour if you
   stopped feeding it. For a typical 200 L barrel (about 0.25 m² of sand
   surface) the slow end is roughly 25 L an hour: **a slow trickle from the
   tap, not a running stream.** Set it with the outlet valve. Faster flow
   means worse filtering.

> **Risk:** Never let the filter run dry. If the sand surface is exposed to
> air for long, the living layer dies and you have to ripen the filter again
> from scratch.

## Cleaning

Over weeks or months the top of the sand clogs and the flow slows down. When
it no longer keeps up with what you need, even with the valve fully open:

1. Let the water level drop to just above the sand (not below it).
2. **Scrape off the top 1–2 cm of sand** with a clean flat tool and set it
   aside to wash for reuse.
3. Refill gently through the diffuser and **ripen it again** — treat the
   output as untrustworthy for the next couple of weeks.
4. After several scrapings, top the bed back up with clean washed sand.

## Storage & distribution

- Store filtered water in clean, covered containers.
- Use a tap or ladle to avoid recontamination by hands.
- Test the output periodically.
