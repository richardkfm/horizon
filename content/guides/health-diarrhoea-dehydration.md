# Treat diarrhoea and dehydration at home

Almost everyone gets a bout of diarrhoea now and then, and most get better on
their own in a few days. What makes it dangerous is **losing water and salts
faster than you take them in** — especially for babies, small children, older
people, and anyone already unwell. The fix is cheap, simple, and saves
millions of lives a year: oral rehydration solution, sipped little and often.

> **Do now:** Start giving fluids as soon as diarrhoea starts — don't wait
> for signs of dehydration. Use ready-made oral rehydration salts (ORS) if you
> have them, or make the home solution below.

## Make oral rehydration solution (ORS)

Ready-made ORS sachets from a pharmacy are best — they have exactly the
right balance, and they keep for years. Mix them with exactly the amount of
clean water the packet says.

If you have no sachets, make the home version recommended by the World
Health Organization:

> **Spec:** **1 litre of clean water** (boiled and cooled, or treated) +
> **6 level teaspoons of sugar** + **½ level teaspoon of salt**. Stir until
> dissolved. Level spoons matter — too much salt makes things worse.

```ascii
     1 litre          6 level tsp        1/2 level tsp
   clean water          sugar               salt
   +---------+      o o o o o o              .
   |         |   +  (flat, not        +   (half,
   |  ~~~~~  |       heaped)              level)
   +---------+
         \_______________ stir ______________/
                           |
                  taste: no saltier than tears
```

teaspoons; if it tastes saltier than tears, add more water*

- **Make it fresh** and use it within 24 hours; cover it and keep it cool.
- **Don't use** fizzy drinks, undiluted fruit juice, or sports drinks
  instead — the sugar balance is wrong and can worsen diarrhoea.

## Give it little and often

- **Small, frequent sips** from a cup or spoon, not big gulps. A stomach
  that's upset keeps down small amounts far better.
- **If they vomit,** wait 5–10 minutes, then start again more slowly — a
  spoonful every minute or two. Most fluid still gets absorbed.
- **How much:** WHO guidance is roughly 50–100 ml (a quarter to half a cup)
  after each loose stool for children under 2, 100–200 ml for children aged
  2–10, and as much as they want for older children and adults.
- **Keep eating** as soon as they feel like it: ordinary, easy food like
  rice, porridge, bananas, soups, and lentils. Food helps the gut recover.

## Babies and young children

- **Keep breastfeeding** — more often than usual, not less. Breast milk is
  food, fluid, and protection at once. Give ORS between feeds as well.
- **Formula-fed babies:** keep feeding formula at its normal strength, made
  with safe water, and give ORS in between. Never water formula down.
- **Health workers often give zinc** to children with diarrhoea, which
  shortens the illness — ask a clinic or pharmacist.
- **Don't give anti-diarrhoea medicines** to children unless a clinician has
  prescribed them.

## Danger signs: get help urgently

> **Risk:** Get medical help straight away if there is:
> - **blood** in the stool
> - **no urine** for 8 hours or more (or very few wet nappies in a baby)
> - **unusual sleepiness, floppiness, or being hard to wake**
> - **sunken eyes**, or a **sunken soft spot** on a baby's head
> - **skin that stays tented** for a moment when gently pinched
> - **unable to drink**, or vomiting everything for several hours
> - **high fever**, severe tummy pain, or diarrhoea lasting more than a few
>   days
>
> Babies under six months, older people, and anyone with a long-term illness
> can go downhill fast — seek help early for them.

Keep giving ORS on the way to help.

## Stop it spreading

Diarrhoea spreads from hands, water, and food. A few habits stop it.

1. **Wash hands with soap** after the toilet, after changing nappies, and
   before preparing food or eating — for 20 seconds, every time.
2. **Make drinking water safe** — see [[water-choosing-treatment]].
3. **The sick person shouldn't cook for others** until 48 hours after their
   last bout of diarrhoea or vomiting.
4. **Separate towels**, and clean toilet seats, taps, and door handles daily.
5. **Handle soiled clothes and bedding carefully** and wash them hot,
   separately.
6. **Keep waste away from water** — see [[survival-sanitation-hygiene]].

> **Tip:** Keep a few ORS sachets in the first-aid kit and the go-bag. They're
> cheap, light, and keep for years.
