# Tie essential knots and lashings

A handful of knots covers most needs: joining ropes, making a loop, securing a
load, and tensioning a shelter. Learn these few well rather than many badly, and
practise until you can tie them in the dark.

> **Rule of thumb:** The best knot is one that holds under load *and* unties
> afterwards. Always leave a "tail" past the knot so it can't slip free.

## The core set

Each knot below has its use, how to tie it, and how to check you tied it
right. "The end" is the short end you work with; "the long part" is the rest
of the rope, the part that takes the load.

### Square (reef) knot

Joins two ropes of the *same* thickness for light loads (bandages, bundles).
Not for heavy loads or for joining different sizes.

1. Hold one end in each hand. Cross the right end over the left and tuck it
   under.
2. Now cross the end in your left hand over the one in your right, and tuck
   it under.
3. Pull all four strands tight.

**Check:** the knot lies flat, and each rope's two strands leave it side by
side. If the ends stick out crosswise, it is a granny knot, which slips —
untie it and start again.

### Sheet bend

Joins two ropes, including *different* thicknesses. More reliable than a
square knot for towing or extending a line.

1. Fold the end of the thicker rope back on itself to make a narrow U.
2. Bring the thinner rope's end up through the U from underneath.
3. Take it round the back of both sides of the U.
4. Tuck the end under its own long part, where that lies across the top of
   the U. Don't pass it back down through the U.
5. Pull tight.

**Check:** the two short ends finish on the same side of the knot. If they
are on opposite sides, retie it, going round the back of the U the other
way.

### Bowline

Makes a fixed loop that doesn't jam and unties easily, even after heavy
load. The rescue knot: a loop you can put around a person or a post.

1. Make a small loop in the long part by laying the rope across itself.
   This is the "hole".
2. Bring the end up through the hole from underneath.
3. Take it round behind the long part.
4. Bring it back down through the hole.
5. Hold the end against the side of the big loop and pull the long part to
   tighten.

The old way to remember it: the rabbit comes up out of the hole, goes round
the tree, and back down the hole.

**Check:** the end finishes inside the big loop with a tail at least a
hand's width long. If the knot falls apart when you pull, the hole was made
the wrong way up — twist it the other way and try again. A bowline can shake
loose when the rope goes slack, so leave a long tail and lock it with an
overhand knot around the loop whenever a person depends on it.

### Two half hitches

Ties a rope to a post, ring, or tree; quick and secure.

1. Pass the end round the post.
2. Take it across the front of the long part, round behind it, and through
   the gap between rope and post. That is one half hitch.
3. Make a second one the same way, a little further from the post, winding
   in the same direction.
4. Slide both snug against the post.

**Check:** the two hitches sit side by side on the long part and both wind
the same way.

### Clove hitch

Starts and ends lashings and ties off to a pole; fast to adjust.

1. Pass the end round the pole.
2. Cross it diagonally over the turn you just made.
3. Take it round the pole again.
4. Tuck the end under the diagonal crossing.
5. Pull both ends to tighten.

**Check:** two turns side by side on the back of the pole, and one diagonal
crossing them on the front. It can roll loose on a smooth pole, so use it to
start and finish lashings, not to hold a load on its own.

### Taut-line hitch

An adjustable loop that grips under tension; perfect for guy lines on a
tarp or tent that you need to tighten.

1. Pass the end round the peg or tree and bring it back beside the long
   part.
2. Wrap the end twice around the long part, working back towards the peg.
3. Carry the end past those two wraps, away from the peg, and tie one half
   hitch around the long part there: once round, in the same direction, with
   the end tucked through.
4. Pull the knot snug.

**Check:** two wraps inside the loop and one outside it. Slide the knot along
the long part to tighten or slacken the line; it should grip when the line
pulls on it.

## Lashings (join poles)

- **Square lashing** — binds two poles crossing at right angles, for frames,
  racks, and shelters. Start with a clove hitch, wrap around the crossing 3-4
  times, add "frapping" turns *between* the poles to tighten, finish with a clove
  hitch.
- **Diagonal lashing** — binds poles that cross at an angle and tend to spring
  apart, e.g. braces. Start with a clove hitch round *both* poles at the
  crossing to pull them together. Wrap 3-4 times across the crossing one
  way, then 3-4 times across it the other way, so the wraps make an X. Add
  2-3 frapping turns between the poles, and finish with a clove hitch on one
  pole.

```ascii
                |
                |   vertical pole
          vvvvv | vvvvv
     ===========+===========    horizontal pole
          ^^^^^ | ^^^^^
                |
                |

   v/^  wraps around the crossing, 3-4 turns each way
   +    frapping turns cinched between the poles pull everything tight
```

## Practice and care

- Practise with cord before you need it; muscle memory matters under stress.
- Keep rope dry and coiled; inspect for fraying before trusting it with weight.
- Never put a knotted rope under load near anyone who could be hurt if it fails.
