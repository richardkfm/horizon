# Care for teeth and toothache without a dentist

Healthy teeth are mostly made at home, not at the dentist: a few minutes of
brushing and cleaning a day, and keeping sugar to mealtimes, prevent most
tooth decay and gum disease. Those habits matter all the more when a dentist
is far away, expensive, or hard to reach — and a toothache can still be
eased until help is available.

> **Do now:** Get urgent medical help — not just a dentist — for **swelling
> of the face, jaw, or neck that's spreading**, swelling that reaches the
> eye, **fever** with a toothache, or **trouble swallowing, breathing, or
> opening the mouth**. A tooth infection that spreads can become
> life-threatening.

## Daily care

1. **Brush twice a day for two minutes** — last thing at night and one other
   time — with a fluoride toothpaste if you have one.
2. **Use a small amount:** a smear for children under three, a pea-sized
   blob for older children and adults. Help children brush until about
   seven or eight.
3. **Spit, don't rinse** after brushing, so the fluoride stays on the teeth.
4. **Clean between teeth once a day** with floss, small brushes, or a thin
   wooden pick — that's where brushing doesn't reach.
5. **Brush along the gum line,** angled gently. Bleeding gums usually mean
   plaque is there; keep cleaning gently and they normally settle within a
   week or two.

## Eat for your teeth

- **Keep sugar to mealtimes.** It's how *often* teeth meet sugar that does the
  damage, not just how much. Sweet snacks and sipped sweet drinks all day are
  the worst.
- **Drink water between meals.** Plain water rinses the mouth; fizzy and
  fruit drinks attack enamel.
- **Crunchy vegetables, nuts, and seeds** are kind snacks. Dried fruit is
  sticky and sugary — keep it to mealtimes.
- **Don't put babies to bed with a bottle** of anything but water.

## When toothpaste runs out: chew sticks

People have cleaned their teeth with chew sticks for thousands of years. The
**miswak** (from the *Salvadora persica* tree) is the best known and is
recognised by the World Health Organization; twigs of neem and some other
trees are used the same way.

1. **Peel about 1 cm of bark** from one end.
2. **Chew that end** until the fibres spread into a soft brush.
3. **Brush** the teeth and along the gum line, as with a toothbrush.
4. **Trim off the used bristles** each day and chew a fresh end.

> **Note:** Chew sticks clean well but don't contain fluoride. Go back to
> fluoride toothpaste when you can get it. Only use twigs you can identify
> with certainty — some plants are poisonous.

## Ease a toothache

- **Rinse with warm salt water:** half a teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm
  water. Swish gently and spit, several times a day and after meals.
- **Clean around the sore tooth** gently — trapped food often causes the pain.
- **A cold pack on the cheek** (a cloth-wrapped cold pack, 15–20 minutes at a
  time) eases pain and swelling.
- **Over-the-counter pain relief** taken as the label says can help. Never
  put a painkiller tablet directly on the gum — it burns.
- **Clove oil** dabbed sparingly on a cotton bud next to the sore tooth can
  numb it for a while. Keep it off the tongue and gums as much as possible,
  and don't use it for young children.
- **Sleep with your head raised** on an extra pillow.

These ease pain; they don't fix the cause. A toothache that keeps coming
back needs a dentist.

## A lost filling or broken tooth

- **Temporary filling material** from a pharmacy (a putty you press into the
  hole) protects the tooth for a few days to weeks. Clean and dry the hole,
  then follow the packet.
- **A crown that falls off:** keep it. A pharmacy dental cement can hold it
  back on temporarily.
- **A sharp broken edge:** cover it with a little temporary filling material
  or dental wax so it doesn't cut the tongue.
- **Chew on the other side** until it's fixed.

## A knocked-out adult tooth

> **Do now:** Pick the tooth up by the crown (the white part), not the root.
> If it's dirty, rinse it briefly in milk or clean water. Push it back into
> its socket if you can and bite gently on a clean cloth, or keep it in milk
> or in the person's own saliva. Get to a dentist within an hour — the sooner,
> the better its chance. Never put a baby tooth back in.

## When it needs a professional

See a dentist or health worker as soon as you can for: pain lasting more than
a couple of days, pain that wakes you at night, a bad taste or pus (a sign of
an abscess), a broken tooth, or a swollen gum. Get urgent help for the signs
in the box at the top.
