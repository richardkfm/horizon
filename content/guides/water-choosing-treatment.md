# Which water treatment should you use?

There is no single "best" way to make water safe — the right choice depends on
**what is wrong with your water**, how much you need, and what you have to hand.
This guide helps you *choose*; the step-by-step builds live in the linked guides.

> **Decide:** Treatment removes different threats. Sediment and cloudiness need
> *filtering*; living things (bacteria, viruses, parasites) need *disinfection*;
> salt, metals and most non-volatile chemicals need *distillation* (fuels and
> solvents need a different source). Most real situations need two steps —
> clear the water first, then disinfect it.

## What is in your water?

| Threat | You might see / suspect | What actually removes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sediment, mud, cloudiness | Murky, coloured, gritty water | Settling, cloth/sand pre-filter |
| Bacteria & parasites | Animal/human activity upstream | Slow sand filter, boiling, chlorine, solar (SODIS) |
| Viruses | Sewage, dense settlement nearby | Boiling, chlorine (not most simple filters) |
| Salt, heavy metals, most non-volatile chemicals | Mining, seawater, runoff | **Distillation** — filtering won't help |
| Fuels, solvents (volatile chemicals) | Fuel smell or sheen, spills, industry | **Nothing simple** — they boil over with the steam, so change source |

> **Risk:** No simple field method removes everything. If you suspect chemical
> or industrial contamination, do not drink it. Distillation handles salt and
> metals, but fuels and solvents boil off with the steam and end up in your
> "clean" water — with any fuel or solvent contamination, find another source.
> When in doubt, treat for living things *and* keep testing.

## Compare the common methods

| Method | Good against | Throughput | Needs | Best when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Boiling** | Bacteria, viruses, parasites | Small batches | Fuel, a pot | You have fuel and need water now |
| **Chlorine / bleach drops** | Bacteria, viruses | Any volume | Unscented bleach, a measure | Treating stored or transported water |
| **SODIS (sun in clear bottles)** | Bacteria, viruses, parasites | Up to 2 L/bottle/day | Clear PET bottles, clear water, strong sun | Hot, sunny climates, no fuel |
| **Slow sand filter** | Bacteria, parasites, cloudiness | Steady for a group | Sand, a barrel, weeks to ripen | A standing supply for ~20 people |
| **Distillation** | Living things, salt, metals, most non-volatile chemicals (not fuels or solvents) | Very small | Heat, condensing surface | Seawater or salty/mineral water |

> **Pick this if:** you must serve a small group day after day from a stream or
> pond — build a **slow sand filter** to clear and biologically treat the water,
> then add a final disinfection step (boil or chlorinate) before drinking.

> **Avoid if:** the water could carry chemicals or salt — sand filters,
> ceramic filters and cloth do nothing for those. Reach for distillation
> (for salt and metals) or change source (always, for fuels and solvents).

## A quick rule of thumb

1. **Cloudy?** Let it settle, then filter through cloth or sand until it runs clear.
2. **From nature?** Assume it carries living things — disinfect by boiling,
   chlorine, or strong sun.
3. **Industry, mining, or sea nearby?** Assume chemicals or salt — distil
   salty or mineral water; if it smells of fuel or solvents, find another
   source.
4. **Unsure?** Combine *clear, then disinfect*, and test the result.

> **Spec:** Bleach: use **plain, unscented household bleach** (no
> fragrance, no "splash-less" or cleaning additives) and check the strength
> on the label. For clear water, add about **2 drops per litre of 5–6%
> bleach** (8 drops per gallon), or about **1–2 drops per litre of 8.25%
> bleach** (6 drops per gallon). Mix, then wait **30 minutes**. It should
> smell faintly of chlorine; if it doesn't, add the same dose again and wait
> another 15 minutes. **Cloudy water:** let it settle and filter it first,
> then use double the dose.
> Boiling: bring to a **rolling boil for 1 minute** (3 minutes above ~2000 m).

> **Tip:** Bleach weakens in storage — replace your bottle every 6–12 months,
> and date it when you buy it.

> **Spec:** SODIS (solar disinfection): use **clear PET plastic bottles of 2
> litres or less**, filled with **clear water** (filter cloudy water first),
> lay them in **full sun for at least 6 hours** — or **2 days in a row** if
> it's cloudy. It doesn't work well in heavy rain; boil or use bleach
> instead.
